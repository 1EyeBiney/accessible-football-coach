Suggestion I approve:
I would make delegation a first-class game mechanic, rather than merely a convenience.
For almost every management system, give the player three levels: automatic, priorities only, and full control. For example, I could tell my OC, “Prioritize film and quarterback development,” and let him allocate his own Focus. I should only have to manipulate his percentages individually if I choose to micromanage.
I'd do the same on game day with three modes for each side of the ball: I call everything; coordinator calls everything; coordinator calls normally but stops for important decisions.
That third option could be excellent. The game automatically advances until something such as third/fourth down, red zone, two-minute situation, a major new matchup discovery, injury, or high-confidence adjustment happens.
That could turn a 130-snap game into perhaps 25–40 meaningful coaching decisions without sacrificing the simulation.

The computer coach is never omniscient. It makes decisions from the same imperfect information model available to the player's staff.
That one requirement will affect a lot of engine decisions later.

Every non-player-controlled team eventually needs some simplified version of coaching, player development, recruiting, staff turnover, game results, roster aging/graduation, coach firing and hiring.
It does not need to use the full human-facing simulation. A lightweight NPC simulation would be better.

One system I would rethink substantially: Legacy perks
The loadout idea is definitely the better choice. 
DESIGN
But I would be careful with permanent raw-power perks such as +5 weekly Focus and “every athletic director gives you another season.”
They create a strange difficulty curve:
A brand-new player gets the hardest version of the game.
An expert who has already mastered it gets increasingly powerful advantages.
Some progression is desirable, but I would make most Legacy unlocks create options rather than power.
Starting at higher levels is good.
Unlocking unusual schools is good.
Unlocking difficult coach backgrounds is good.
Unlocking new staff archetypes is good.
Unlocking historical career summaries, challenge runs and alternate starting conditions is good.
If you retain raw advantages, the loadout should have a point cap. Perhaps the player owns twenty perks but can equip only three or spend only ten loadout points on a run.
Then the meta-game becomes another choice rather than permanent easy mode.

The MVP I would build
I would resist implementing the entire career design first.
The first genuinely useful prototype only needs:
One high-school team, one opponent, one roster, one OC, one DC, a small playbook, one weekly Focus screen, scouting/film, one game, the hunch system, the counter system, stamina/injuries, and a postgame report.
No recruiting.
No NIL.
No NFL.
No legacy system.
No job market.
No remote AI.
If that one game is fun—specifically, if I find myself thinking, “My OC says their corner is vulnerable, but I think they're about to roll the safety over there, so I'm going to hit the seam instead”—you have the heart of the entire product.
Then everything else in this 700-line design gives that game meaning over a career.


finally, let's address this first as this is the reason for the game:
2. 
The actual football resolution model is still the largest mechanical hole.
The document says outcomes depend upon matchups, Execution, Scheme, and risk, but there isn't yet a definition of how a snap actually resolves. 
DESIGN
Before building the career systems, I would design a small playable football engine answering questions like:
What determines whether pressure gets home? What determines completion versus breakup versus interception? What creates rushing yardage? What determines YAC? When do sacks, fumbles and penalties occur? How do blocking matchups interact with the intended target matchup? What makes one play a good counter to a particular coverage/front rather than simply having higher Execution?
I don't think it needs hundreds of football attributes. Quite the opposite. But it needs a clear interaction model underneath plays.
Something like:
Situation + play concept + execution + relevant player matchups + defensive call + live state + randomness = result.
Most importantly, different play concepts need genuine reasons to exist. Otherwise the sophisticated coaching layer eventually collapses into, “Find whichever matchup currently has the highest edge and attack it.”
