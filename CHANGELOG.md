# Changelog

## 0.3.0, August 27, 2026. Save and load, the fourth down flow, and a tuning pass.

Save and load (DESIGN.md 21.10): a game in progress survives a page reload and can be written to and read from a file. engine/save.js serialises a controller to plain JSON, tagging every player reference outside the roster that owns him (a hunch's target, an event's carrier or tackler, a belief store's evidence) so loading rebuilds real shared object identity rather than disconnected copies - the substitution flow mutates a player reference directly, so getting this wrong would have meant a substitution made after a reload silently stopped showing up anywhere else. G saves to a file; Shift G and the main menu's Load open the native file picker; the main menu's Resume reads a crash copy now written to localStorage after every decision point rather than never.

The fourth down flow (DESIGN.md 8.4): the coach's own fourth down now goes through the same suggest-and-accept grammar as offense and defense, with confidence wording and F for the full list of punt, field goal, go for it, and a fake of whichever kick is recommended. Built without a fourth full coordinator - a fake is a real conversion attempt with a kick formation's dressing on it, not a separate probability model, and the situational math (engine/game.js's fourthDownConfidence) is deterministic rather than drawn from a belief store, argued in DESIGN_PROPOSALS.md proposal 3. The opponent's own fourth down and a victory-formation kneel-out are never a question the coach is asked, at any delegation setting. Onside kicks and a kickoff-specific choice are not built.

A tuning pass on three items from ISSUES.md. Cover three (DESIGN.md 26.2, Decided): the defensive caller no longer gives it a flat, dominant base weight; it now earns its way up on first down, short yardage, and heavy personnel, with two-high shells the clear answer on long yardage. Its run-support bonus was raised to match cover zero's, and the two largest positive matrix entries against it were trimmed by two points each, the cap the ruling allows. Call rate came down from 27.7% of pass snaps to 23.7%. Blowouts: games decided by more than thirty-five points came down from 18.8% to 8.7% of mixed-quality games (target about one in eight) through a narrower harness quality range, a leading team running the ball more at a lower bar, and a trailing team no longer gambling on fourth-and-short deep in its own territory before it is genuinely desperate. Fumbles lost were measured and deliberately left below their target, for the same reason the previous balance pass already gave.

Two accessibility auditor passes over the new screens, one after save/load and one after the fourth down flow, found and fixed five real things: a stale confirmation left open across an asynchronous load could have silently discarded the resumed game; the container's own focus trap was fighting the native file picker it is meant to allow; no reentrancy guard on the save/load key; resuming a finished game spoke a stale down and distance instead of the final and postgame review; and the F12 keyboard explorer described F on a fourth-down suggestion as opening the offensive formation list, because it was mode-aware but not step-aware. Full findings in REVIEW_NOTES.md.

node test/run.js: 344 checks across six files (was 262 at 0.2.0). node harness.js and node matrix.js numbers, before and after every tuning change, are in BALANCE_NOTES.md.

## 0.2.0, August 27, 2026. The staff, the controller, and the first playable shell.

The game opens in a browser and plays. Press Enter on the one button and a full game can be coached from kickoff to the postgame review without a mouse.

Staff (engine/staff.js): staff members with attributes on the same scale as players, a belief store per staff member per game fed only by the events on a play result and by pre-game scouting, and hunches in a fixed shape carrying a source, a kind, a target, a confidence word, a recommendation, its evidence, an urgency, and a plain sentence with no numbers in it. Evaluation sets a per-matchup bias drawn once a game that never averages away, and how many looks the coordinator needs before he speaks. Communication sets how long after that he takes to say it. Both automatic coaches now decide from beliefs rather than from raw counters, so the counter loop runs off what the defensive coordinator believes he has seen, and a coordinator who has not worked it out never adjusts. Substitution recommendations, the spotter watching the other sideline, the trainer's first read on an injury, and recovered players announced at a change of possession are all in.

Two supporting engine changes, both argued in DESIGN_PROPOSALS.md. The quarterback now works the concept's progression in order and throws the first read that is open enough, rather than always finding the most open receiver; without this a concept's read order means nothing and a bracket costs the defense nothing. And defensive staffs now have coverage tendencies by down and distance, without which there is nothing for a coordinator to read.

Controller (engine/controller.js): the only surface the interface talks to. It says what input is needed next, gives either coordinator's suggestion with the confidence in words and the word sub when the call changes personnel, takes the coach's call, and hands back announcements with a priority and a source for the chime. The report loop of section 19.3 lives here, along with the three delegation modes, the play clock, halftime, and the postgame staff review. engine/game.js was refactored into startGame and stepGame so a game can be walked one snap at a time.

Interface (index.html, ui/, main.js): the Accessible Golf model. One application container, one focus trap, one keydown listener with ordered interceptors, one live region, everything sanitised for speech, chimes synthesised per source, help split by mode with audible headings, and the F12 keyboard explorer. Screens: boot, main menu, a team choice between two authored high school programmes described entirely in words, a pre-game screen, and the game screen with the play call flow of section 16.5. Terse and full verbosity, and the pacing setting, both work.

Ten findings from the simulation reviewer were fixed. The largest: every defensive adjustment now costs the defense somewhere else, as section 8.3 requires. Bracketing the top receiver used to change Four Verticals by a tenth of a yard and now costs the offense four and a half. Also fixed: substituting mid-drive was free and never gave the defense its free substitution; offensive holding could erase an interception; plays wiped out by a penalty still counted in the box score, which inflated every passing number; two per cent of kickoffs reached the opponent's half narrated as ordinary returns; the injury roll read base rather than effective toughness; the quarterback sneak could not fumble or be held; the heavy personnel group drew a light box against nearly everything and the matrix check mislabelled its own box cells so it could not see that; the blitz separation bonus was paid twice; and screens were sack-proof by exemption rather than by consequence. Two more were in the controller: a halftime choice rerolled the coordinator's evaluation bias, and the halftime briefing read the opposing coordinator's private hunch.

Harness numbers with equal teams over 300 games, per team per game: 20 points, 67 plays, 59 percent completion, 6.8 yards per attempt, 4.5 yards per carry (5.5 light box, 4.6 normal, 2.4 loaded), sacks 5 percent of dropbacks, interceptions 2.3 percent of attempts, 5.6 penalties, 4.8 punts, 0.3 injuries. The scheme matrix is sharper than it was: Four Verticals spreads about nine yards between its best and worst coverage against six and a half at 0.1.0, and the Play Action Shot spreads about eight and a half.

The hunch model passes its test. At the final whistle, a poor offensive coordinator names the receiver who really was winning about thirty-nine percent of the time, an average one about fifty-three percent, and a good one about seventy-five percent, and the man a good one names is on average less than a yard of edge away from the best one against three and a half for a poor one. Turning that read into points is worth a little under three points a game, which is real but quieter than the design reads as intending; that is an open question in DESIGN_PROPOSALS.md.

Tests: test/run.js with four files and 244 checks, covering seeded replay, the belief model, the hunch contract, the announce queue, menu wrap, grid edges, the interceptor firewall, the Focus allocation rule, a whole game played through the controller, and a whole game played through the interface by keystroke.

Known gaps, next: saving and loading a whole run, the weekly Focus screen, the call sheet built the night before, scouting and film, the pre-game hunches from scouting rather than from nothing, and the career around all of it.

## 0.1.0, August 27, 2026. First engine and harness.

Engine: seeded RNG, attributes and two-platoon roster generator with talent bands, four formations and seventeen concepts with an authored scheme matrix, six fronts, six coverages, four pressures, six adjustments, phase-chain snap resolution for passes and runs with pressure timing, coverage assignment, quarterback decision errors, run after catch, blocking at the point of attack, broken tackles and breakaways, penalties, fumbles, and events. Game loop with the high school clock, downs, punts, field goals, kickoffs, extra points and two-point tries, stamina, injuries, automatic coaches, the substitution rule with the no-huddle gamble, the exploitation counter driving bracket, help, and load adjustments, timeouts, victory formation, the mercy running clock, and the ten-yard-line overtime.

Harness numbers with equal teams over 300 games, per team per game: 21 points, 68 plays, 60 percent completion, 7.2 yards per attempt, 4.4 yards per carry (5.4 light box, 2.6 loaded), sacks 5 percent of dropbacks, interceptions 2 percent of attempts, 5.5 penalties, 0.3 injuries. Matrix check: Four Verticals 12.5 yards per snap against cover three and 4.5 against two-man; Play Action Shot 10.3 against cover three and 1.9 against cover four; the quick game gains against six rushers while the deep shots are sacked.

Known gaps, next: the observed-events knowledge model for coordinators (hunches), the human play-calling hooks, the report loop, halftime, the postgame staff review, and the weekly screen.
